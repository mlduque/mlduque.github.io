Blocklists: how to know if you're on one and how to get off

What email blocklists are, which ones really matter and the process to get delisted without being listed again.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-14

In this guide
  1. What a blocklist is
  2. Which ones matter
  3. How to know if you're listed
  4. How to get off

What a blocklist is

A blocklist (blacklist or DNSBL) is a database of IPs and domains that have sent spam. Many mail servers check it before accepting an email: if your IP or domain is listed, they reject your emails or send them to spam.

Which ones matter

There are hundreds, but only a few have real impact:

  • Spamhaus (ZEN, SBL, XBL, PBL and DBL): the most widely used in the world. Being listed here is serious.
  • Barracuda: widely used by companies.
  • SpamCop: based on user reports, and listings expire on their own.
  • The internal lists of Microsoft, Gmail and Yahoo, which aren't public but show up in their bounces.

Other small lists barely affect delivery: don't obsess over them.

How to know if you're listed

The signs: bounces with text like «blocked using», «listed», «Spamhaus» or «block list», or a sudden drop in opens. Check your sending IP and domain in «My domain» or in the full audit, and paste any bounce into the bounce translator.

How to get off

  1. Find out why: the list's page usually explains it (spam traps, infected computer, home IP…).
  2. Fix the cause: clean your list, remove inactive contacts, review forms without double opt-in, secure your server.
  3. Request delisting on the list's website. If you use a sending platform and the IP is theirs, ask them.
  4. Come back carefully: send to your most active contacts first.

Requesting delisting without fixing the cause only gets you listed again, and it gets harder each time.
