DKIM2: what comes after DKIM

What DKIM2 is, which DKIM problems it solves (replay, forwarding and fake bounces), where the standard stands and what you should do today.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-16

In this guide
  1. What DKIM2 is
  2. The problems it aims to solve
  3. How it works
  4. What changes for senders
  5. What to do today

What DKIM2 is

DKIM2 is the next version of DKIM, the digital signature that proves an email was sent by your domain and that nobody changed it. It's being prepared by the IETF DKIM working group, the body that publishes Internet standards, with authors from Yahoo, Google and Fastmail.

Important: it isn't a standard yet. It's a draft (the main specification is at version 09, from November 2026, alongside two other documents on best practices and DNS records) and no provider requires it. Good old DKIM is still the one that counts.

The problems it aims to solve

  • Replay: someone gets hold of a legitimate email signed by a domain with a good reputation and resends it to millions of people. The DKIM signature is still valid, so the spam takes advantage of someone else's reputation.
  • Forwarding and mailing lists: when an intermediary changes the subject or adds a footer, the signature breaks. ARC was a patch for this, but it depends on the receiver trusting whoever forwards.
  • Fake bounces (backscatter): errors that reach innocent domains whose name was used in a forged send.
  • No feedback: DKIM has no standard way to tell the sender what happened to their email.

How it works

  • Every hop signs: each server handling the email adds its own DKIM2-Signature header with a sequence number, leaving a verifiable chain of custody.
  • It signs the envelope: each signature includes the real sender and recipients of the connection (mf= and rt=). If someone resends the email to other people, it won't match and the replay is detected.
  • Reversible changes: a Message-Instance header stores «recipes» to undo each intermediary's changes and verify the original signature.
  • Bounces only along the path: error notices are only accepted if they travel back along the same chain, which cuts backscatter.
  • Keys: they're published in the same place as now (selector._domainkey.yourdomain) and it supports RSA, Ed25519 and a post-quantum algorithm (ML-DSA-44).

What changes for senders

The most practical consequence: in DKIM2 the signing domain must match the envelope sender (the Return-Path). If your platform sends with its own bounce domain today, with DKIM2 you'll need to use one of yours. That's exactly what you should already do to align SPF with DMARC.

DKIM and DKIM2 will coexist for years, and ARC will probably disappear once DKIM2 spreads.

What to do today

  • Don't change your infrastructure for a draft: wait until it's an RFC and Gmail, Yahoo or Microsoft announce they use it.
  • Keep DKIM spotless: 2048-bit keys (or Ed25519 as well as RSA), signing with your own domain and rotating them regularly.
  • Set up your own bounce domain on every platform: it aligns SPF now and prepares you for DKIM2.
  • Take DMARC to p=quarantine or p=reject.
  • Ask your sending platform about its DKIM2 plans and follow the news at dkim2.eu and in the IETF DKIM working group.
