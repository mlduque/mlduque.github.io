What email HTML should look like to avoid the spam folder

The technical rules for building an email that reaches the inbox and looks right in Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail and Yahoo: sender, subject, size, tables, images, links, unsubscribe and dark mode.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-20

Sender, subject line and preheader

  • Sender with a recognisable name, not in capitals, and able to receive replies (better not to use «noreply»).
  • Subject line of 30 to 50 characters, not in capitals, with at most one exclamation mark, few emojis and no fake «RE:» or «FW:».
  • No tricks such as repeated currency symbols or disguised words (fr33).
  • Preheader of 40 to 90 characters that complements the subject, hidden at the start of the HTML.

Size and structure

  • Under 102 KB: Gmail clips heavier emails, and the cut often removes the unsubscribe link.
  • <style> block under 16 KB, or Gmail discards it.
  • Under 5,000 px tall, or the Outlook app cuts it with «Read more».
  • Table-based layout (role="presentation"), 600 px wide, with a fixed container for Outlook using conditional comments.
  • Inline styles for everything important, in case the email client removes the <style>.
  • Plain text version: filters penalise HTML-only emails.

What doesn't work in email clients

  • Never: scripts, iframes, forms, video, external stylesheets, SVG, base64, WebP or AVIF images.
  • Avoid: flexbox, grid, position, web fonts, CSS variables, calc(), animations, :hover and the <button> element.
  • Use with care: background images, media queries, gradients, transparency, rounded corners and shadows. They work in some clients but not others.

Images

  • 60/40 rule: at least 60% text versus 40% image. An email that is a single image is typical of spam.
  • Alt text on every image with content: many people have images blocked.
  • width and height attributes, display:block, border:0 and height:auto.
  • Hosted at a public https URL, never attached.
  • Don't put important text inside an image.

Links

  • Always https.
  • No shorteners or links to IP addresses.
  • Link text must match its destination: showing one site and going to another is a phishing technique.
  • Few links and one clear main call to action.
  • Your own tracking domain, better than the platform's shared one.

Buttons and text

  • HTML buttons, not images: a link inside a cell with a background colour, visible even with images blocked.
  • Enough contrast between button text and background.
  • Text of at least 16 px with a line height of 1.5 for easy reading on mobile.
  • Safe fonts (Arial, Helvetica, Verdana, Georgia…).
  • No hidden text or text the same colour as the background, and few capitals and exclamation marks.

Unsubscribe and legal details

  • A visible unsubscribe link in the body of the email.
  • One-click unsubscribe in the headers (List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post), required by Gmail and Yahoo. Generate yours with One-click unsubscribe.
  • Company name and postal address in the footer.
  • Why the person is receiving the email and a link to the privacy policy.

Dark mode and accessibility

  • Some clients invert colours: use logos with a transparent background and check that everything stays readable.
  • If you want to control dark mode, define styles with @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark).
  • Make sure buttons and text can be told apart without relying on colour alone (colour blindness) and that the email doesn't break at 200% zoom.

You can test all of this in the advanced preview in My campaign, or build the email directly with the newsletter builder, which already applies these rules.

Checklist

  • ☐ Sender, subject line and preheader reviewed.
  • ☐ Under 102 KB and 5,000 px tall.
  • ☐ Tables, 600 px and inline styles.
  • ☐ Plain text version.
  • ☐ 60/40 rule and alt on images.
  • ☐ Links with https, no shorteners and consistent.
  • ☐ HTML buttons with good contrast.
  • ☐ Unsubscribe link, one-click unsubscribe and postal address.
  • ☐ Tested with images off and in dark mode.
