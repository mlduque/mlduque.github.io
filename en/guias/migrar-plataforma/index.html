How to switch sending platforms without losing your reputation

What to prepare before migrating to a new ESP or your own servers, how to move authentication, contacts and unsubscribes, and how to warm up the new platform.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-20

In this guide

  2. Before migrating
  3. Authentication on the new platform
  4. Warm up the new platform

  6. Checklist

Why a migration can go wrong

When you switch sending platforms (from one ESP to another, or from an ESP to your own servers), the IPs change, sometimes the link domains too, and so does the way emails are signed. To Gmail, Microsoft or Yahoo you're almost a new sender, even though your brand is the same.

Well planned, a migration doesn't have to be noticeable. Done badly, it can send your emails to spam for weeks.

Before migrating

  • Export everything: contacts, unsubscribes, hard bounces, complaints and last open or click dates.
  • Respect unsubscribes: import unsubscribes, bounces and complaints as suppressed in the new platform. Writing again to people who unsubscribed is the fastest way to pile up complaints.
  • Inventory your sends: list everything sent from the old platform (newsletters, automations, transactional emails).
  • Don't cut over at once: keep the old platform active for a few weeks while you warm up the new one.

Authentication on the new platform

  1. Add the new platform to your SPF without removing the old one until you've finished. Watch out for the 10-lookup limit.
  2. Set up DKIM with your domain (not the platform's), so the reputation stays yours.
  3. Set the Return-Path or bounce domain to a subdomain of yours, so SPF is aligned.
  4. If the platform uses a link tracking domain, customise it with a subdomain of yours.
  5. Check the DMARC reports: the new platform should appear passing alignment.

Check the result in Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft requirements.

Warm up the new platform

New IPs have no history. If you have a dedicated IP, do a warm-up of 3 to 6 weeks starting with your most active contacts. If you use the platform's shared IPs, the risk is lower, but it's still wise to start with the most active contacts and ramp up over one or two weeks.

Meanwhile, the old platform keeps sending to the rest of the list and you gradually move volume from one to the other.

After migrating

  • Check bounces, complaints and opens per provider every day during the first month.
  • Compare deliverability with the previous platform: if a provider gets worse, slow down volume to it.
  • Once all volume is on the new platform, remove the old one from SPF.
  • Keep the export of unsubscribes and bounces from the old platform.

Checklist

  • ☐ I've exported contacts, unsubscribes, bounces and complaints.
  • ☐ Unsubscribes and bounces imported as suppressed.
  • ☐ SPF, DKIM, Return-Path and DMARC aligned on the new platform.
  • ☐ Warm-up plan starting with the most active contacts.
  • ☐ The old platform stays active until the move is finished.
