What to do if your emails go to spam: how to recover your domain's reputation

There's no button to restore your reputation, but there is a method. What sinks it, what not to do and the step-by-step plan to get back to the inbox.

How to tell it's a reputation problem

When your domain or IP reputation drops, the symptoms are usually these:

  • Opens drop suddenly, especially at one provider (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo).
  • Your tests land in the spam folder even though the content hasn't changed.
  • Block bounces appear, such as 550 5.7.1, 421 4.7.0 or Microsoft's S3150.
  • Your sending platform warns you about complaints or limits your account.

If any of this is happening, upload your last campaign's bounces to the bounce report: it will tell you whether the problem is your list, your reputation or something technical.

Why reputation drops

It's almost always an accumulation of bad practices, not a single send:

  • Sending a lot of volume to people who are no longer interested and don't open your emails.
  • Being marked as spam (complaints).
  • Hitting spam traps: abandoned addresses or addresses created to catch bought or poorly maintained lists.
  • Bought or old lists, or ones collected without clear consent.
  • Increasing volume suddenly, or changing IP or platform without warming it up.
  • Badly built HTML or irrelevant content, which filters also penalise.
  • Authentication failures (SPF, DKIM or DMARC).

Recovering your reputation isn't mechanical or instant: you get there by applying good practices day by day, step by step and with patience.

What not to do: change domain

It's the most common temptation and one of the worst mistakes: switching to another domain, subdomain or IP to «start from scratch» while doing the same things.

The new sender's reputation will have the same problems and drop again in the short term or, at best, the medium term. Providers also detect these changes and see them as an attempt to dodge filters.

The only solution that works is to improve engagement: getting the people who receive your emails to open them, click and not mark them as spam.

Step 1: stop sending to people who don't read you

Split your list by each contact's activity. Any platform that shows the date of the last open or click will do:

  • Active: opened or clicked in the last 30 to 90 days.
  • Less active: engage now and then, for example between 90 and 180 days.
  • Inactive: haven't opened in more than 180 days or have never opened any of your emails.

If your reputation has dropped, the first thing to do is stop sending to inactive contacts for a few weeks. They show no interest and very probably already get your emails in spam: every send to them costs you reputation.

Trust clicks more than opens: Apple Mail opens emails automatically and makes some people look active when they aren't.

Step 2: send only to your active contacts

For at least two or three weeks, send only to active contacts. They're the ones who can improve your reputation, because they open, click and don't complain.

In this phase, remember the basics:

  • Send less volume and don't flood inboxes. Start with part of your usual volume and increase it gradually.
  • Avoid anything that looks like spam: exaggerated subject lines, too many images, shortened links or heavy HTML.
  • Take care of the content: make it relevant and useful to the recipient.

Step 3: gradually bring everyone else back

When results with active contacts are good, add the less active ones in batches. They don't engage often, but they're not lost.

Then send inactive contacts a re-engagement (win-back) campaign in small batches: two or three emails asking whether they want to keep hearing from you. Remove anyone who doesn't respond.

If complaints, bounces or blocks rise at any point, go back to the previous step.

Meanwhile: clean up and check

  • Clean your list: remove hard bounces and misspelled addresses, and validate doubtful contacts. You can do it with the database tool.
  • Check authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC in Check my domain.
  • Make unsubscribing easy: a visible link and one-click unsubscribe. If unsubscribing is hard, people click «Report spam».
  • Review the HTML of your campaigns in My campaign.

Bear in mind that hitting a spam trap can affect your reputation for months, and the IPs you send from too. Even if you remove it after cleaning your list, recovery takes time.

How to measure recovery

  • Google Postmaster Tools: watch the spam rate (below 0.1% and never above 0.3%) and the requirements compliance dashboard. The new version no longer shows domain or IP reputation, and the data arrives a few days late.
  • Microsoft SNDS: shows the status of your IPs with Outlook and Hotmail.
  • Your metrics by provider: compare opens, clicks, bounces and complaints at Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo separately.
  • The bounce report after every campaign, to check that blocks are going down.

If you want a plan tailored to your case, with the steps for each week, use the plan to recover your reputation.

Checklist

  • Don't change domain, subdomain or IP to escape the problem.
  • Pause sending to inactive contacts.
  • Send only to active contacts for 2 or 3 weeks, with less volume.
  • Clean your list and check SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
  • Take care of content and HTML.
  • Bring the less active back in batches.
  • Run a re-engagement campaign to inactive contacts and remove anyone who doesn't respond.
  • Measure in Postmaster Tools, SNDS and by provider before increasing volume.
  • Be patient: reputation recovers in weeks or months, not days.
