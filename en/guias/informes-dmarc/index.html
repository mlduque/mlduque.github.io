How to read DMARC reports (and get to p=reject)

What DMARC aggregate reports (RUA) are, what data they contain, how to spot who sends on your behalf and how to move from p=none to p=reject without losing emails.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-17

In this guide
  1. What DMARC reports are
  2. What a report contains
  3. How to interpret it
  4. From p=none to p=reject step by step
  5. Common mistakes
  6. Checklist

What DMARC reports are

When your DMARC record includes rua=mailto:…, the big providers (Gmail, Yahoo, Microsoft and others) send you a daily report with every email they received using your domain: from which IP, how many and whether they passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

It's the only way to know who sends on your behalf: your legitimate platforms, the ones you've forgotten and those trying to impersonate you.

What a report contains

It arrives as a compressed XML file (.zip or .gz) attached to an email. Inside there is, for each IP that sent mail:

  • The source IP and how many emails it sent.
  • The SPF and DKIM results, and with which domain.
  • Whether they passed alignment with the sender's domain.
  • What the provider did with them under your policy (delivered, sent to spam or rejected).

Reading the XML by hand is tedious. Upload it to the DMARC report analyser and it tells you which sources are legitimate, which fail and which look like spoofing.

How to interpret it

  • Legitimate sources that pass: all good.
  • Legitimate sources that fail: for example, your CRM or invoicing tool sends with your domain but has no DKIM set up. These are the ones to fix before tightening the policy.
  • Unknown sources that fail: possible spoofing. DMARC at quarantine or reject is exactly what stops them.
  • Forwarding: emails forwarded by mailing lists or users often fail SPF. If DKIM passes, there's no problem.

From p=none to p=reject step by step

  1. p=none with rua: you only observe. Keep it for 2 to 4 weeks and note every legitimate source.
  2. Set up aligned SPF and DKIM on every legitimate source, until almost all your mail passes.
  3. p=quarantine with pct=25, then 50 and 100: failing mail goes to spam. Watch the reports at each step.
  4. p=reject: failing mail is rejected. Your domain is protected against spoofing.

Generate the record for each step with the SPF, DMARC and BIMI generator.

Common mistakes

  • Moving to reject without checking the reports: you stop delivering invoices, notifications or CRM emails.
  • No rua: you have DMARC but don't know what's happening.
  • Forgetting subdomains: use sp= if you send from them.
  • Receiving reports in your personal inbox: many arrive every day. Use a dedicated address.

Checklist

  • ☐ My DMARC has rua=mailto: with a dedicated address.
  • ☐ I review the reports every week.
  • ☐ I know all my legitimate sources.
  • ☐ They all pass aligned SPF or DKIM.
  • ☐ I have a plan to reach p=reject.
