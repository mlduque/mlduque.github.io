Why your emails go to spam: 10 causes and how to fix them

The most common reasons emails end up in the spam folder and what to do about each one.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-14

In this guide
  1. First of all: it's not just the content
  2. Technical causes
  3. List causes
  4. Content causes
  5. Rescue plan

First of all: it's not just the content

For years people thought words like «free» sent emails to spam. Today Gmail's and Outlook's filters mainly look at who you are (authentication and reputation) and how your recipients react (opens, replies, complaints). Content matters, but less.

Technical causes

  1. Missing SPF, DKIM or DMARC, or not aligned with your domain.
  2. You're on a blocklist (Spamhaus, Barracuda…).
  3. A new IP or domain sending a lot at once, without a warm-up.
  4. A shared IP with low-quality senders.

List causes

  1. Inactive contacts: mailing people who haven't opened in months tells the provider your emails aren't wanted.
  2. Invalid addresses and spam traps: typical of old or bought lists.
  3. Many complaints: if marking as spam is easier than unsubscribing, people will do it.

Content causes

  1. Links to domains with a bad reputation or URL shorteners.
  2. An email that's a single image with no real text, or HTML over 102 KB.
  3. Misleading subject lines, capitals, lots of exclamation marks or typical spam words.

Rescue plan

  1. Check your domain and the blocklists.
  2. Audit a real email before sending it.
  3. For 2 to 4 weeks, send only to contacts who opened or clicked in the last 90 days.
  4. Make unsubscribing visible and add one-click unsubscribe.
  5. Watch Google Postmaster Tools and Microsoft SNDS, and increase volume gradually.
