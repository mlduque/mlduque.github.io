What your sending platform should check before letting you send

The checks a serious ESP does (or should do) when you open your account, before each send and afterwards. It helps you prepare and understand why they ask for what they ask for.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-20

Why your platform asks for so much

Sending platforms (ESPs) share IPs and reputation across many customers. If a single customer sends spam, it can hurt delivery for everyone. That's why serious platforms review each sender before letting them send and monitor them afterwards.

Knowing these checks tells you what to prepare, helps you avoid unexpected account blocks and lets you tell a serious platform from one that doesn't look after its reputation.

1. At sign-up: who you are

  • A real company: legal name, tax ID, address and a contact person.
  • An active website with real content that matches the declared activity.
  • Sender domain: it must be yours, not days or weeks old, not on blocklists or flagged as dangerous, and not imitating a well-known brand.
  • Sector: payday loans, gambling, crypto, pharmacy and similar usually go through manual review.
  • Expected volume and frequency, to decide between shared or dedicated IPs and plan the warm-up.
  • Acceptance of the acceptable use policy and anti-spam rules.

2. Where your contacts come from

This is the most important question. A serious platform will want to know:

  • How they subscribed: website form, shop, event… and whether you can prove consent (date, source).
  • Whether you use double opt-in, the safest way to avoid spam traps.
  • How old the list is and when you last sent to it.

Purchased, rented or scraped lists are rejected. Read the guide on spam traps to understand why.

3. Technical setup before the first send

  • SPF including the platform, without exceeding 10 lookups.
  • DKIM signed with your domain, with a 2048-bit key.
  • DMARC published, at least with p=none and rua, and SPF or DKIM aligned with your sender.
  • Bounce domain (Return-Path) on a subdomain of yours.
  • Your own link tracking domain, not the platform's shared one.
  • One-click unsubscribe with the List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers.
  • A real sender that receives replies.

Check everything with Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft requirements.

4. List and content review

  • List validation: misspelt addresses, non-existent domains, role, disposable and duplicate addresses. You can do it yourself beforehand with My database.
  • Limited or manually reviewed first sends for new accounts.
  • Links: no shorteners, no blocklisted or newly registered domains.
  • Phishing: no logos or text from banks, couriers or brands that aren't yours.
  • Content: SpamAssassin-style test, text-to-image ratio and legal details (sender, address and unsubscribe link). Check it in My campaign.

5. Monitoring after every send

Serious platforms monitor every campaign and may pause your account if certain limits are exceeded. The usual ones are:

  • Hard bounces: above 2%, alert.
  • Spam complaints: above 0.1%, alert; above 0.3%, pause.
  • Spam traps and blocklist listings.
  • Gmail and Microsoft data (Postmaster Tools and SNDS) and provider deferrals.
  • Sudden changes in volume, content or list type.

If your account is paused, the platform will ask to review the cause with you before reactivating it. It isn't a punishment: it protects your reputation and that of other customers.

Checklist for senders

  • ☐ Company details and active website ready.
  • ☐ My own domain, with some age and clean.
  • ☐ I can prove where my contacts come from and their consent.
  • ☐ SPF, DKIM, DMARC, Return-Path and link domain set up.
  • ☐ One-click unsubscribe and a visible unsubscribe link.
  • ☐ List validated before importing it.
  • ☐ Campaigns reviewed before sending.
  • ☐ I know which bounce and complaint limits my platform monitors.
