Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft requirements for bulk senders

The rules you must follow since 2024 if you send a lot of email, and how to check you meet them.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-14

In this guide
  1. Who counts as a bulk sender
  2. Mandatory authentication
  3. One-click unsubscribe
  4. Spam complaint rate
  5. What happens if you don't comply

Who counts as a bulk sender

Gmail and Yahoo apply stricter requirements to anyone sending more than 5,000 emails a day to their users. Microsoft (Outlook.com, Hotmail and Live) has applied the same threshold since May 2025. Once you cross it, you're considered a bulk sender permanently.

Even if you send less, meeting these requirements is the best way to reach the inbox: filters reward those who do.

Mandatory authentication

  • SPF and DKIM set up for your domain.
  • DMARC published, with at least p=none.
  • Alignment: the «From» domain must match the SPF or the DKIM domain.
  • Valid reverse DNS (PTR) on the sending IPs.
  • An encrypted TLS connection.

For all senders, even small ones, Gmail requires at least SPF or DKIM.

One-click unsubscribe

Commercial emails must include the List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click headers, which allow unsubscribing with one click from the provider's interface, plus a visible unsubscribe link in the email. Unsubscribes must be processed within 2 days.

Most email marketing platforms already add these headers: check it with the full audit.

Spam complaint rate

Gmail asks you to keep the complaint rate below 0.1% and never reach 0.3%. That is, fewer than 1 complaint per 1,000 delivered emails. You can see it in Google Postmaster Tools.

To keep it low: only send to people who gave you permission, stop mailing inactive contacts, make unsubscribing easy and deliver what you promised at sign-up.

What happens if you don't comply

Emails that don't meet the authentication rules are rejected with errors such as 550 5.7.26 (Gmail) or 550 5.7.515 (Microsoft), or deferred with temporary errors. If your complaint rate is high, your emails will go to spam even if everything else is right. Use the bounce translator to interpret any error you receive.
