Why your emails go to spam in Gmail (and how to stop it)

The real reasons Gmail sends your emails to spam or rejects them, what to check in Postmaster Tools and a checklist to get back into the inbox.

Gmail decides by reputation

Gmail has no public blocklist and no form to ask to be taken out of spam. It decides based on what its users do with your emails: whether they open them, reply, move them to the inbox or mark them as spam. That's your reputation, and both your domain and your IP have one.

That's why what works with Gmail isn't «asking for forgiveness» but showing for a few weeks that people want your emails.

The 6 most common causes

  1. Incomplete authentication: without aligned SPF, DKIM and DMARC, Gmail is suspicious. If you send more than 5,000 emails a day to Gmail, it's also mandatory.
  2. Too many complaints: Gmail asks you to stay below 0.1% and never reach 0.3%. Above that, emails go to spam or get rejected.
  3. No one-click unsubscribe: marketing emails must include the List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers.
  4. Poor list quality: purchased, old or inactive contacts. Lots of sends to mailboxes nobody opens drag reputation down.
  5. Sudden changes: a new domain or IP sending a lot at once, or a volume spike without warm-up.
  6. Suspicious content: shortened links, link domains with poor reputation, lots of images and little text, or misleading subject lines.

Check the data in Postmaster Tools

Google Postmaster Tools is free and the only way to see what Gmail thinks of you. Verify your domain (with a TXT record) and check:

  • Spam rate: the percentage of emails users mark as spam. It's the most important figure.
  • Compliance status: whether your domain meets the bulk sender requirements (authentication, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, spam rate).
  • Authentication and encryption: what share of your mail passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC and arrives over TLS.

Postmaster only shows data if you send enough daily volume to Gmail. If you see nothing, that doesn't mean something is wrong.

If Gmail rejects your emails

Besides sending to spam, Gmail rejects mail that doesn't comply. The most common bounces are:

  • 550 5.7.26: the email fails authentication (DMARC, SPF or DKIM).
  • 421 4.7.28 or 550 5.7.1 mentioning an «unusual rate» or spam: Gmail is throttling or rejecting because of reputation or unusual volume.
  • 421 4.7.0 related to TLS or the IP's reverse DNS.

Paste the bounce into the bounce translator to see exactly what it's asking for.

How to get back into the inbox

  1. Fix the technical side: check your domain in Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft requirements.
  2. For 2 to 4 weeks, send only to contacts who opened or clicked in the last 60 to 90 days.
  3. Lower volume and increase it again gradually.
  4. Make the unsubscribe link easy to see: it's better for someone to unsubscribe than to mark you as spam.
  5. Check every campaign in My campaign before sending.
  6. Watch the spam rate in Postmaster Tools every few days.

Gmail reputation improves slowly: it's normal to take several weeks to see results.

Myths that don't help

  • «Switching domains will fix it»: a new domain starts with no reputation and Gmail is just as wary. And if you repeat the same habits, the new one will end up the same.
  • «Banned words send me to spam»: content matters much less than reputation. A flawless email from a sender with a bad reputation still goes to spam.
  • «More volume means more sales»: sending to people who don't open lowers your reputation and hurts every other send.

Checklist

  • ☐ SPF, DKIM and DMARC aligned with the sender's domain.
  • ☐ One-click unsubscribe (List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post).
  • ☐ Spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.1%.
  • ☐ I only send to active contacts who gave permission.
  • ☐ IP with correct reverse DNS and sending over TLS.
  • ☐ I've checked the campaign's content and links.
