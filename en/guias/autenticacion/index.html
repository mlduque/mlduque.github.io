SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained without jargon

What the three records that decide whether your emails can be trusted are, how they work together and in what order to set them up.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-14

In this guide
  1. The problem they solve
  2. SPF: the guest list
  3. DKIM: the email's signature
  4. DMARC: the one that sets the rules
  5. In what order to set them up

The problem they solve

Email was invented with no way of checking who sends each message: anyone can type your address in the «From» field. SPF, DKIM and DMARC are three records you publish in your domain's DNS so that Gmail, Outlook or Yahoo can check that an email claiming to come from you was really sent by you.

Since 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require them from bulk senders, and Microsoft joined in 2025. Without them, your emails end up in spam or are rejected outright.

SPF: the guest list

SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a list of the servers authorised to send email with your domain. When an email arrives, the receiving server looks at the IP it was sent from and checks whether it's on your list.

An example: v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:sendgrid.net ~all means «Google and SendGrid may send; anyone else is suspicious».

Two golden rules: a domain can only have one SPF record, and it can't need more than 10 DNS lookups. Go over and SPF fails.

DKIM: the email's signature

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a digital signature to every email. Your platform signs with a private key and you publish the public key in your DNS. The recipient checks the signature and learns two things: that the email was sent by someone authorised by your domain, and that nobody changed it along the way.

Unlike SPF, DKIM survives forwarding, which makes it the most reliable of the three. Use 2048-bit keys and make sure each tool signs with your domain, not theirs.

DMARC: the one that sets the rules

DMARC brings the other two together and adds the key piece: alignment. An email passes DMARC if SPF or DKIM pass with the same domain shown in the «From». DMARC also says what to do with emails that fail:

  • p=none: just monitor and send you reports.
  • p=quarantine: send them to spam.
  • p=reject: reject them.

And the most useful part: with the rua tag you receive daily reports of every server sending with your domain.

In what order to set them up

  1. List everything that sends email with your domain: company email, newsletter, shop, CRM, invoicing, website.
  2. Create a single SPF that includes them all.
  3. Turn on DKIM with your domain in every tool.
  4. Publish DMARC with p=none and a rua address.
  5. Review the reports for 2 to 4 weeks and fix what fails.
  6. Move up to p=quarantine and, later, to p=reject.
