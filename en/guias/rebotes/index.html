Bounces: hard, soft and what to do with each

How to read a bounce, how the types differ and when you should remove a contact from your list.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-14

In this guide
  1. What a bounce is
  2. Hard bounces
  3. Soft bounces
  4. Blocks: the third type
  5. Benchmarks

What a bounce is

A bounce is the reply from a server that couldn't deliver your email. It always carries a code: a basic three-digit one (such as 550) and usually an extended one (such as 5.1.1). The first digit says almost everything: 4 is temporary and 5 is permanent.

Hard bounces

They're permanent: the mailbox doesn't exist (5.1.1), the domain doesn't exist (5.1.2) or the account is disabled (5.2.1). The only right response is to remove the address straight away. Continuing to send to non-existent addresses is one of the signals that hurts your reputation most.

Soft bounces

They're temporary: full mailbox (4.2.2), busy server, greylisting or rate limits (4.7.0). Your platform retries them. If an address soft-bounces on 3 to 5 sends in a row, treat it as hard.

Blocks: the third type

Many 5.7.x bounces have nothing to do with the recipient: the server rejects you because of your reputation, a blocklist or an unauthenticated domain. Here you shouldn't remove the contact, but fix the problem. If you classify them as hard bounces, you'll end up deleting good addresses.

Benchmarks

  • Total bounce rate per send: below 2%.
  • Hard bounces: ideally below 0.5%.
  • If a send goes over 5%, stop and review the list before continuing.
