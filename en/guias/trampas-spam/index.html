Spam traps: what they are and how to avoid them

What spam traps are (pristine, recycled and typo traps), how they end up on your list and how to clean your database so you don't hit them.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-20

In this guide

  2. The three types
  3. How to know if you have them
  4. How to avoid them
  5. If you've already hit one
  6. Checklist

What a spam trap is

A spam trap is an email address that doesn't belong to a real person and has never subscribed to anything. Mailbox providers and blocklists use them to detect who sends without permission or with poorly maintained lists.

If you send to a trap, it doesn't bounce or complain: it just gets recorded. A few are enough for your IP or domain to land on a blocklist or lose reputation.

The three types

  • Pristine: addresses created only to catch spammers, hidden on websites. They only reach your list if you buy databases or scrape emails from websites without permission. They're the most serious.
  • Recycled: addresses that were real, were abandoned, deactivated by the provider and later reactivated as traps. They reach your list if you keep sending to contacts who haven't opened anything in years.
  • Typo traps: addresses like @gmial.com or @hotmial.com. They get in when someone mistypes their email in a form.

How to know if you have them

Nobody tells you which addresses are traps: if it were known, spammers would remove them. The indirect signs are:

  • You land on a blocklist without having changed anything.
  • Microsoft SNDS shows «trap hits» on your IPs.
  • You have many contacts who never open or click.
  • Your list has addresses with misspelt domains.

Analyse your list in My database: it detects misspelt domains, role addresses, disposable addresses and duplicates.

How to avoid them

  1. Never buy or rent lists.
  2. Double opt-in: confirm every sign-up with a verification email. A trap will never confirm.
  3. Validate in the form: flag mistakes like «did you mean gmail.com?» before saving.
  4. Inactive policy: if a contact hasn't opened or clicked in 6 to 12 months, send a re-engagement campaign and, if they don't respond, stop sending to them.
  5. Remove hard bounces on the first bounce.

If you've already hit one

  • Separate contacts with no recent activity and stop sending to them.
  • Check where your latest contacts came from (forms, imports, events).
  • Check blocklists in My domain and follow the guides to get off them.
  • For a few weeks, send only to your most active contacts.

Checklist

  • ☐ I don't use purchased lists or addresses collected without permission.
  • ☐ I use double opt-in or, at least, validate emails at sign-up.
  • ☐ I fix or remove misspelt domains.
  • ☐ I stop sending to people who haven't opened in 6 to 12 months.
  • ☐ I remove hard bounces right away.
