How to get off Spamhaus: SBL, CSS, XBL, PBL and DBL

What each Spamhaus list means, how to find out which one you're on, which ones you can remove yourself and what to do so you don't get listed again.

By María Luisa Duque · Email Deliverability Manager · Updated 2026-11-20

In this guide

  2. The lists, one by one
  3. Find out where you are
  4. How to get off each list

Why Spamhaus matters so much

Spamhaus is the most widely used blocklist organisation in the world. Thousands of mailbox providers and companies check its lists before accepting an email. If your IP or domain appears on them, many of your sends will bounce with messages mentioning spamhaus.org.

It isn't a single list: there are several, and each is resolved differently. The first step is knowing which one you're on.

The lists, one by one

  • SBL: IPs Spamhaus links to spam or to senders with a bad reputation. It's the most serious one and it's reviewed by a person.
  • CSS: IPs sending mail with technical or quality problems: wrong HELO, generic reverse DNS, typical spam content. It's the most common one for misconfigured legitimate servers.
  • XBL: IPs of infected machines, open proxies or compromised servers.
  • PBL: IP ranges whose owner (for example, a home internet provider) declares they shouldn't send mail directly. It's not a penalty, it's a policy.
  • DBL: domains, not IPs. It lists domains used in spam or phishing, including the domains in your emails' links.

Find out where you are

  1. Go to Spamhaus's official checker, check.spamhaus.org.
  2. Enter your sending server's IP or your domain.
  3. It tells you which list you're on, since when and often why.

You can also check your IP and domain in IP address information and My domain, which query several lists at once.

How to get off each list

  • CSS and XBL: you can usually request removal yourself from the Spamhaus checker. First fix the cause: a HELO with a real name, its own reverse DNS pointing to the IP, and make sure the IP isn't sending spam from an infected machine. If you don't fix it, you'll be listed again.
  • PBL: if your IP is from a home range, the right thing is to send through your provider's server or a sending platform, not directly. If it's your real mail server, with a fixed IP and its own reverse DNS, you can ask for it to be removed from the PBL from the checker.
  • SBL: a person at Spamhaus reviews it. Usually the IP owner (your hosting provider or sending platform) has to contact them explaining what has been fixed.
  • DBL: check the domains in your links and your sender. If a domain was compromised, clean it up and request removal from the checker.

Why you can get listed without being a spammer

  • Sending to old or purchased lists that contain spam traps.
  • A server that introduces itself with a wrong HELO or without its own reverse DNS.
  • An unprotected web form that bots use to send spam from your server.
  • An infected company computer or email account.
  • Sharing an IP with other customers who send spam.

Read the HELO, PTR and NAT guide to understand the technical requirements Spamhaus checks.

Checklist

  • ☐ I know which list I'm on (SBL, CSS, XBL, PBL or DBL).
  • ☐ I've found and fixed the cause.
  • ☐ HELO and reverse DNS are correct.
  • ☐ I've requested removal (myself or through my provider, depending on the list).
  • ☐ I monitor my IPs and domains to find out if I'm listed again.
