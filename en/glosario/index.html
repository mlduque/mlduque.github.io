Email deliverability glossary

The key email deliverability terms explained in one or two sentences: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, bounces, blocklists, reputation and more.

Alignment
Match between the «From» domain and the domain authenticated by SPF (Return-Path) or DKIM (d=). DMARC only passes if at least one of the two is aligned.
ARC
Authentication chain that preserves SPF and DKIM results when an email passes through intermediaries such as mailing lists or forwarding.
BIMI
Standard that shows your brand logo next to your emails in the inbox. It requires DMARC at quarantine or reject and, in Gmail and Apple, a VMC or CMC certificate.
Blocklist (DNSBL)
Database of IPs and domains that have sent spam, which servers check before accepting an email. The most influential are Spamhaus and Barracuda.
Complaint rate
Percentage of recipients who mark your email as spam. Gmail asks you to keep it below 0.1% and never reach 0.3%.
Dedicated IP
Sending IP used only by you. Your reputation depends only on you, but it needs steady volume and an initial warm-up.
Delivery vs deliverability
The delivery rate measures emails the server accepts; deliverability, those reaching the inbox. 99% delivery can hide a lot of spam.
DKIM
Digital signature your platform adds to every email, which the recipient verifies with the public key published in your DNS. It proves the email is yours and hasn't been altered.
DMARC
Policy saying what to do with emails that fail aligned SPF and DKIM (nothing, spam or reject) and where to send reports. Mandatory for bulk sending to Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft.
Domain reputation
Score for the sending domain, independent of the IP. Today it weighs more than IP reputation, especially at Gmail, and follows you even if you change platform.
Double opt-in
Two-step sign-up: the user subscribes and confirms with a click in an email. It prevents fake addresses, typos and spam traps.
Engagement
How recipients interact with your emails: opens, clicks, replies or moving them out of spam. It's one of the signals filters rely on most.
ESP
Email Service Provider: the platform you send your campaigns with, such as Mailchimp, Brevo or emBlue.
FCrDNS
Round-trip check: the IP resolves to a name (PTR) and that name resolves back to the same IP. It's a Gmail requirement for bulk senders.
Feedback loop (FBL)
Service through which a provider notifies you each time a user marks your email as spam, so you can unsubscribe them.
Google Postmaster Tools
Free Google tool showing your domain and IP reputation, spam rate and authentication for your sending to Gmail.
Greylisting
Anti-spam technique that temporarily rejects an unknown sender's first attempt. Legitimate servers retry and the email is delivered minutes later.
Hard bounce
Permanent rejection (5.x.x address or mailbox codes): the mailbox or domain doesn't exist. The address must be removed straight away.
Inbox placement
Percentage of emails reaching the inbox, as opposed to spam or other tabs. It's the real measure of deliverability.
IP reputation
Score providers give a sending IP based on its history: volume, complaints, bounces and spam traps.
List hygiene
Regular database maintenance: removing bounces, inactive contacts, duplicates and role addresses to protect your reputation.
Microsoft SNDS
Free Microsoft service with data on the reputation and complaints of your sending IPs at Outlook.com and Hotmail.
MTA
Mail Transfer Agent: the server software that sends and receives emails, such as Postfix, PowerMTA or Exchange.
MTA-STS
Policy that forces servers sending you email to use TLS with a valid certificate, so nobody can intercept the mail.
MX record
DNS record stating which servers receive a domain's mail. Without MX, a domain can't receive emails or bounces.
One-click unsubscribe (List-Unsubscribe)
Headers that let people unsubscribe from the provider's own interface with a single click. Mandatory for bulk senders at Gmail and Yahoo.
PTR (reverse DNS)
Record linking an IP to a server name. Providers reject or distrust emails sent from IPs without a PTR or with a generic PTR.
Return-Path
Technical address bounces go back to, also called «envelope from» or «MAIL FROM». It's the domain SPF checks and doesn't always match the visible «From».
Role address
Generic address like info@, sales@ or admin@, usually read by more than one person. They generate more complaints and some platforms don't allow mailing them.
Seed list
Set of test mailboxes at different providers used to check whether a send lands in the inbox, promotions or spam.
Shared IP
IP used by several customers of the same platform. Recommended for low volumes, but the reputation also depends on the others.
SMTP code
Three-digit number a server replies with (250 accepted, 4xx temporary, 5xx permanent), often together with an extended code like 5.1.1.
Soft bounce
Temporary rejection (4.x.x codes): full mailbox, busy server or rate limit. It's retried and only removed if it happens repeatedly.
Spam trap
Address that belongs to nobody and is used to catch senders with bad practices. Mailing it points to a bought, old or non-double-opt-in list.
Spamhaus
Organisation that runs the most widely used blocklists (ZEN, SBL, XBL, PBL and DBL). Being listed causes rejections on a large share of the world's servers.
SPF
DNS record listing the servers authorised to send email with your domain. A domain can only have one, and it can't exceed 10 DNS lookups.
Throttling
Limiting sending speed, whether imposed by the receiving provider or set by you so as not to overload it.
TLS
Encryption of the connection between mail servers. It protects emails in transit and is mandatory for Gmail bulk senders.
VMC and CMC
Certificates proving a logo belongs to a brand, needed for BIMI to work in Gmail and Apple Mail. A VMC requires a registered trademark; a CMC, a year of public use of the logo.
Warm-up
Gradually increasing sending volume when starting with a new domain or IP, to build reputation without looking like spam.
